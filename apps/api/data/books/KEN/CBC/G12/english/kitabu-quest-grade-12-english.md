## Answer And Teacher Notes

A strong English answer should:

- match the purpose and audience;
- use evidence from the passage, discussion, task, or real context;
- organise ideas into clear sentences and paragraphs;
- choose words that fit the tone;
- edit spelling, punctuation, grammar, and meaning after feedback.

For reading questions, use point, evidence, and explanation. For writing tasks, plan before writing and revise at least one sentence before submitting.

Answer-key guide: reading answers need a point, a quoted or paraphrased detail, and an explanation. Grammar answers need the corrected sentence and the reason for the correction. Writing answers need purpose, audience, structure, and editing evidence. Oral tasks need a clear position, reason, example, and respectful close.

Rubric for self-checking:
- 4: The answer addresses the exact question, uses subject vocabulary correctly, gives evidence or working, and includes a useful check or correction.
- 3: The main answer is correct but one part needs stronger evidence, clearer working, better labels, or a fuller explanation.
- 2: Some understanding is shown, but the answer is incomplete, too general, or hard to follow.
- 1: The answer is guessed, off topic, or missing the evidence needed for the task.

Correction routine: reread the command word, underline the key data or evidence, improve one weak step, and write one sentence explaining why the final answer is reasonable.
