## Answer And Teacher Notes

A strong Mathematics answer should:

- state what is known and what must be found;
- choose a suitable model, table, diagram, expression, equation, graph, or calculation;
- show working step by step without hidden jumps;
- label the final answer and check it in context;
- explain one possible error and how to avoid it.

For proof, algebra, geometry, data, and measurement work, the method matters as much as the final answer. A learner should be able to read the working and understand why each step is valid.

Answer-key guide: calculation tasks should include the formula or rule used, substitution, simplified working, units, and a check. Geometry tasks should include a labelled diagram or named theorem. Algebra tasks should include expansion, collection of like terms, solving steps, and substitution checks. Data tasks should show totals, counts, scale, and interpretation.

Rubric for self-checking:
- 4: The answer addresses the exact question, uses subject vocabulary correctly, gives evidence or working, and includes a useful check or correction.
- 3: The main answer is correct but one part needs stronger evidence, clearer working, better labels, or a fuller explanation.
- 2: Some understanding is shown, but the answer is incomplete, too general, or hard to follow.
- 1: The answer is guessed, off topic, or missing the evidence needed for the task.

Correction routine: reread the command word, underline the key data or evidence, improve one weak step, and write one sentence explaining why the final answer is reasonable.
