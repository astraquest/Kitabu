## Laboratory safety rules and measurements of physical quantities: Learn

Laboratory safety rules and measurements of physical quantities begins with safe behaviour and accurate measurement.

Core idea:
- Physics uses measurements such as length, mass, time, temperature, and volume.
- A measurement is useful only when the number and unit are both written.

## Qualitative analysis of linear motion: Learn

Qualitative analysis of linear motion explains how position changes with time.

Core idea:
- Motion means an object changes position compared with a reference point.
- Distance tells how far an object moves.
- Time tells how long the motion takes.
- Speed compares distance and time.
- A distance-time table or graph can show whether motion is slow, fast, or changing.

## Qualitative analysis of linear motion: Worked Example

Worked example for Qualitative analysis of linear motion: A cart moves 12 metres in 4 seconds. Find its speed.

1. Speed = distance / time.
2. Distance = 12 m and time = 4 s.
3. 12 / 4 = 3.

Answer: the cart moves at 3 m/s.

## Force (I): Learn

Force (I) explains pushes, pulls, and changes in motion.

Core idea:
- A force can start motion, stop motion, change speed, change direction, or change shape.
- Forces are measured in newtons.
- Balanced forces do not change motion.
- Unbalanced forces can make an object accelerate, slow down, or turn.

## Force (I): Worked Example

Worked example for Force (I): A learner pushes a box, and the box starts moving.

Observation: the push is an unbalanced force.

Explanation:
1. The box was at rest.
2. The push acted on the box.
3. The box changed its motion, so the force had an effect.

Conclusion: forces can change the motion of objects.

## Newton's Laws of Motion (I): Learn

Newton's Laws of Motion (I) explains how forces affect motion using Newton's laws.

Core idea:
- First law: an object stays at rest or keeps moving in a straight line unless an unbalanced force acts on it.
- Second law: a larger force causes a larger acceleration, while a larger mass is harder to accelerate.
- Third law: when one object exerts a force on another, the second object exerts an equal and opposite force.
- Force diagrams help you show direction and balance.

## Newton's Laws of Motion (I): Worked Example

Worked example for Newton's Laws of Motion (I): Explain why a football at rest starts moving when kicked.

1. Before the kick, the ball is at rest.
2. The foot applies an unbalanced force on the ball.
3. Because of Newton's first law, the ball changes its state of motion only when the force acts.
4. The ball also pushes back on the foot with an equal and opposite force, showing Newton's third law.

Conclusion: the kick changes the motion because it provides an unbalanced force.

## Centre of gravity: Learn

Centre of gravity explains balance and stability.

Core idea:
- Centre of gravity is the point where the weight of an object seems to act.
- A low centre of gravity usually makes an object more stable.
- A wide base of support helps an object resist toppling.
- You can investigate balance using safe objects such as rulers, cardboard shapes, and books.

## Centre of gravity: Worked Example

Investigation model for Centre of gravity: Balance a ruler on one finger.

1. Place a ruler across one finger.
2. Move your finger slowly until the ruler balances.
3. Mark the balance point.
4. Add a small rubber on one side and observe the new balance point.

Conclusion: adding mass changes where the centre of gravity acts.
