## Graphs of linear motion: Learn

Graphs of linear motion explains how position changes with time.

Core idea:
- Motion means an object changes position compared with a reference point.
- Distance tells how far an object moves.
- Time tells how long the motion takes.
- Speed compares distance and time.
- A distance-time table or graph can show whether motion is slow, fast, or changing.

## Graphs of linear motion: Worked Example

Worked example for Graphs of linear motion: A cart moves 12 metres in 4 seconds. Find its speed.

1. Speed = distance / time.
2. Distance = 12 m and time = 4 s.
3. 12 / 4 = 3.

Answer: the cart moves at 3 m/s.

## Friction force and Newton's laws of motion: Learn

Friction force and Newton's laws of motion explains how forces affect motion using Newton's laws.

Core idea:
- First law: an object stays at rest or keeps moving in a straight line unless an unbalanced force acts on it.
- Second law: a larger force causes a larger acceleration, while a larger mass is harder to accelerate.
- Third law: when one object exerts a force on another, the second object exerts an equal and opposite force.
- Force diagrams help you show direction and balance.

## Friction force and Newton's laws of motion: Worked Example

Worked example for Friction force and Newton's laws of motion: Explain why a football at rest starts moving when kicked.

1. Before the kick, the ball is at rest.
2. The foot applies an unbalanced force on the ball.
3. Because of Newton's first law, the ball changes its state of motion only when the force acts.
4. The ball also pushes back on the foot with an equal and opposite force, showing Newton's third law.

Conclusion: the kick changes the motion because it provides an unbalanced force.
