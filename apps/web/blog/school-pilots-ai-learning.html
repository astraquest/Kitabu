How schools can pilot AI learning safely

Kitabu AI team · Updated 4 July 2026

A school pilot should prove learner value while protecting teachers, students, data, and classroom trust.

Start with a narrow learning goal

The safest pilot begins with a clear goal: improve homework completion, support weak maths topics, add evening practice, or give teachers earlier visibility of who’s falling behind. Avoid launching every feature at once — one goal, one term, honest numbers.

Keep teachers in the loop

AI should not replace teachers. It should help them see who is struggling, where, and what kind of support may help — while taking routine marking off their plates so their time goes where judgment matters. A pilot that teachers experience as relief succeeds; one they experience as surveillance fails. More on the teacher experience: Kitabu for teachers.

Use the right controls

Schools should ask hard questions before any pilot: Is the space guided and age-appropriate? Is parent consent collected for under-18s? Does the provider comply with the Kenya Data Protection Act? Can accounts be deleted? Kitabu’s answers are on our safety page — bring the same questions to any tool you evaluate.

Measure the pilot honestly

Measure learner activation, minutes revised, topics improved, teacher workload, and parent feedback. If the pilot improves learning visibility without creating operational chaos, it is ready to scale — and if the numbers don’t move, stop. That’s why Kitabu’s 30-day whole-school pilot is free: the numbers should make the decision.
