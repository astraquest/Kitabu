Why maths gaps grow so fast

Maths is cumulative. Fractions feed decimals; decimals feed percentages; percentages feed half of Grade 8. When a child misses one idea in Week 2, every later topic that depends on it gets harder — and by exam time the child believes "I’m bad at maths." Usually they’re not. They’re missing one or two foundations, and nobody has had time to find which ones.

How Kitabu revises maths differently

  • The check-up comes first. A free ≈15-minute diagnostic finds the exact strands where the foundation is missing — not just "weak in maths."
  • Explanations go step by step, the CBC way. The working is shown the way the teacher expects it, in English or Kiswahili. Your child can ask "why?" at every line.
  • Practice until it holds. Short quizzes after every explanation confirm the idea stuck before moving on — and weak topics come back for another pass at the right time.
  • You see the climb. "Improved from 42% to 68% on decimals" on your WhatsApp every Friday. That’s how you know the revision is real.
