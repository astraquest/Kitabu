Grade 6 · KPSEA

KPSEA revision for Grade 6 — structured, calm, and visible.

KPSEA is your child’s first national assessment. The goal isn’t panic revision in the last month — it’s steady coverage, discovered gaps, and confidence.

What KPSEA actually assesses

The Kenya Primary School Education Assessment at the end of Grade 6 covers Mathematics, English, Kiswahili, Integrated Science (Science & Technology), and the humanities learning areas. It’s designed to check competency — what a learner can do with what they know — which means understanding beats memorisation.

A sensible KPSEA revision plan

  • Start with a check-up, not a timetable. Kitabu’s free ≈15-minute diagnostic shows which strands are solid and which need work, so revision time goes where it matters.
  • Short, regular sessions. Twenty focused minutes an evening beats a three-hour weekend marathon. The tutor explains, the quiz confirms, the weak topic returns later for another pass.
  • All learning areas, one place. Maths, English, Kiswahili, Science, Social Studies — with explanations in English or Kiswahili.
  • Parents see the countdown. Every Friday, a WhatsApp message tells you which areas improved and which still need attention — while there’s still time to act.

When should KPSEA revision start?

Earlier and lighter is better than late and heavy. Steady 20-minute sessions from the start of Grade 6 beat a final-month panic — and the diagnostic tells you where to begin.

Does Kitabu cover all KPSEA learning areas?

Yes — Mathematics, English, Kiswahili, Integrated Science, and Social Studies, aligned to the CBC syllabus for upper primary.

Can my child revise offline?

Downloaded books and saved lessons work offline — useful for evenings when bundles run out. The live tutor needs a connection.
